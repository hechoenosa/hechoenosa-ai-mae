# Ideas, people and plans

Plain explanations for Week 3 and beyond. Same words as the two Hecho en Osa one-pagers. Explain one idea at a time, with the example. Never do the Builder's thinking for them: ask a question that helps them start.

## Page 1 · Find a real problem and the people who have it

### Problem
A problem is something that makes life harder for real people, often. Not "the world needs an app".
- Write: "My problem is ___."
- Check 4 things: Real (it happens to real people) · Matters (it helps others, not just me) · We can ask (I know someone who has it) · Small enough (I can help with it before Demo Day).
- Example: trash on the beach.

### 5 Whys
The first problem is usually the surface. Ask "Why?" and write each answer above the last. Stop when it clicks, usually at Why 3 or 4. That's the REAL problem.
- Example: Trash on the beach → Why? No bins nearby → Why? Nobody empties them → REAL: No one is in charge of the bins.

### Persona
One made-up person who has your problem. "Everyone" is no one.
- Name, age, something they'd say. What frustrates them. What they need. Where to find someone like them.
- Example: Rosa, 45, runs a soda by the beach. "I pick up trash every morning before I open."
- Why we need a persona: it stops you building for "everybody". Every choice becomes "Would Rosa use this?" It also tells you who to interview.

### User interviews
Talk to people like your persona to learn about THEIR life. Not to sell your idea.
- Ask about their life, not your idea.
- Ask about the last time it happened, not "would you…".
- Talk less, listen more. Write their exact words.
- Good questions: What does a normal day look like for you? What's the hardest part of [the problem]? Tell me about the last time it happened. How do you handle it today? What's annoying about that? Then: "Why?" or "Tell me more."
- Never ask: "Would you use my app?" or "Is my idea good?" People say yes to be nice.
- 2 people, 10 minutes or more each. Write down who (first name or role), their exact words, and what surprised you.
- Everyone says "great idea!"? You explained your idea too early. Go back to asking about their life.

### Talking to strangers
It's normal to feel nervous. Do it scared.
- Start with people your family knows. Then someone new, with a parent next to you.
- Open simple: "Hi, I'm working on a school project about [problem]. Can I ask you 3 quick questions?"
- If they say no, that's fine. Say thank you and ask the next person.
- Safety: in public or at home with family around, a parent nearby, first names only, ask before recording, never go somewhere alone with someone.

## Page 2 · Decide what to build

### First principles
Break a problem down to what is really true, then build up from there. Don't copy what everyone else does.
- Ask: What do we know for sure? What are we only assuming?
- Example: "We need an app for the trash." True: the bins fill up and nobody empties them. Maybe a paper list on the soda's wall solves it faster than an app.
- The 5 Whys is one way to do it.

### MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
The smallest version of your product that still helps your persona. Not the dream version.
- If your product could only do ONE thing, what would it be?
- If your sentence has "and", it's two things. Pick one.
- Example: "It reminds whoever's turn it is to empty the bins." Not: reminds AND tracks AND rewards AND has a map.
- Ugly and working beats beautiful and half-done.

### PRD (Product Requirements Document)
One page that says WHAT you're building and FOR WHOM. Not how. The how (code, tools) comes later in the TRD.
Our PRD has these parts, in this order:
1. Project name
2. Our idea, in one sentence
3. The problem
4. 5 Whys and the REAL problem
5. Persona
6. Interviews: who we talked to (first name or role), their exact words, and what we learned
7. The one thing (our MVP)
- Tip: write it by hand or in plain words first. It becomes your first prompt for AI later.
