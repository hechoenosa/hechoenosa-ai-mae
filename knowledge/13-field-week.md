# Field week · going out to ask real people

How to make the interviews in Week 3 go well. Builders are 11–17. Be warm and brave-making: "Do it scared" is the program's first rule. Never write their questions, answers, problem or persona for them: give the method and one example, then ask them a question back.

## Safety first (always say the relevant part)
- An adult they trust is always nearby: a parent or another family adult. Never alone with someone they don't know.
- Public places or home with family around. Never get in a car or go into someone's house or shop back room.
- First names only. Never ask for or write down last names, phone numbers, addresses or photos of faces. Don't give theirs either.
- Ask before recording. If the person says no, just take notes.
- Anyone makes them uncomfortable: stop, say "Thank you!", walk away, tell their adult. They never owe anyone an interview.
- Online interviews: only with a parent in the room.
- If a Builder says something unsafe happened, follow the Safety section in the rules.

## Who to interview
People like the persona. Start easy, then go a little braver:
1. Family at home (family counts!).
2. People your family knows: neighbours, family friends, the people at the soda or pulpería you always go to.
3. Someone new, with your adult right next to you.
- Can't find anyone like the persona: ask a family adult who they know with that problem, or ask your mentor.
- Everyone you know is too similar: that's fine for Week 3. Two real conversations beat zero perfect ones.

## Before you go (5 minutes)
- Write 5 questions in your notebook, in your own words.
- Practice once with someone at home.
- Pen and notebook, or a notes app. Phone charged if you'll record.
- Your opener ready: "Hi, I'm doing a school project about [problem]. Can I ask you 3 quick questions?" (Spanish: "Hola, hago un proyecto sobre [problema]. ¿Le puedo hacer 3 preguntas rápidas?") Use "usted" with grown-ups you don't know.

## The questions (from the interview worksheet; pick 5)
1. Tell me about your week. What does a normal day look like?
2. What's the hardest part of [the problem]?
3. Tell me about the last time that happened. What did you do?
4. How do you handle it today? An app, WhatsApp, paper, asking someone?
5. What's annoying about the way you do it now?
Never: "Would you use this?" or "Do you like my idea?" People say yes to be nice.

## Asking "Why?" without sounding rude
When they say something interesting, dig one level deeper. Kind ways to say "why":
- "Why is that?" · "Can you tell me more?" · "What happened next?" · "When was the last time?" · "How did that feel?"
- Spanish: "¿Por qué?" · "¿Me cuenta más?" · "¿Y qué pasó después?" · "¿Cuándo fue la última vez?"
- Smile, nod, then wait. A short silence makes people keep talking. Talk less, listen more.
- Asking "why" 3 or 4 times in a row is the 5 Whys, live.

## During the interview
- Ask about the past, not the future: "last time" beats "would you".
- Write their exact words in quotes. One great quote is gold for the Week 3 form.
- Don't explain your idea. If they ask, say "I'll tell you at the end!"
- 10 minutes is enough. End with: "Thank you so much! This really helps."

## Right after (while you remember)
Write three things for each person: who (first name or role, like "Don Carlos, fisherman"), their exact words in quotes, what surprised you.
Then ask yourself: does my problem still look the same? Does my persona still fit? Changing it is a WIN, not a fail. That's what interviews are for.

## When it goes wrong (all normal)
- Too nervous to start: start with family, use the opener word for word, bring your adult. Do it scared.
- They said no: "No problem, thank you!" and try the next person. A no is not about you.
- One-word answers: ask "Tell me about the last time…" or "Can you show me?"
- Everyone says "great idea!": you explained your idea too early. Next time ask only about their life.
- They talked about a different problem: write it down. Maybe it's the real one.
- The interview showed nobody has the problem: great discovery. Try the next problem from your list, or make it smaller. The fit check worksheet helps.
- Forgot to write quotes: write what you remember right now, in their words as best you can, and say so.
- Only got 1 interview: send the form with what you have, and keep going.
- They speak another language: ask in the language they're most comfortable in; write quotes in that language. Spanish is fine on the form.

## Turning notes into the form
Problem → 5 Whys → persona → 2 interviews (who, quote, surprise) → the one thing → promise. Every box of the Week 3 form is already started: they fill in the blanks from their notebook.
